01 Implement several new public-holiday calendars across three new/existing modules of the workalendar package. 1363 words · source specification

Implement several new public-holiday calendars across three new/existing modules of the workalendar package. All calendars follow the existing framework conventions: each is a class exposing a holidays_set(year) method that returns a set of datetime.date objects for the holidays of that year. Existing base-class functionality (holidays_set, add_working_days, working-day computations, weekend/shift helpers) is available for reuse.

The specific public API surface and behavior required is described below.

1. workalendar/africa.py (new module)

SouthAfricaCalendar

holidays_set(year) must include the following holidays.

Fixed-date holidays (every year):

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • March 21 — Human Rights Day
  • April 27 — Freedom Day
  • May 1 — Workers' Day (Labour Day)
  • June 16 — Youth Day
  • August 9 — National Women's Day
  • September 24 — Heritage Day
  • December 16 — Day of Reconciliation
  • December 25 — Christmas Day
  • December 26 — Day of Goodwill

Easter-based holidays:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)

Sunday-shift rule: Any of the above holidays that falls on a Sunday is additionally observed on the following Monday (the Monday must also be added to the set).

Concrete consequences that must hold:

  • For 2013: June 16 (Youth Day) is a Sunday, so June 17 must also be present.
  • For 2014: April 27 (Freedom Day) is a Sunday, so April 28 must also be present.

2. workalendar/europe.py (existing module — add classes)

UnitedKingdomCalendar

holidays_set(year) must include:

Fixed-date holidays:

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • December 25 — Christmas Day
  • December 26 — Boxing Day

Easter-based holidays:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)
  • Easter Sunday
  • Easter Monday (Monday after Easter Sunday)

Variable-date bank holidays:

  • Early May Bank Holiday — first Monday of May
  • Spring Bank Holiday — last Monday of May
  • Late Summer Bank Holiday — last Monday of August

Shift rule for New Year, Christmas and Boxing Day: When one of these fixed holidays falls on a weekend, an additional observed holiday is added on the next available weekday, cascading so that no two observed days collide. Concretely:

  • If January 1 falls on a Sunday, January 2 is also a holiday (e.g. 2012).
  • When December 25 is a Sunday: December 25 and December 26 remain holidays, and additionally December 26 shifts to December 27 (so 2011 must contain December 25, 26 and 27).

(The May/summer bank holidays are always defined on a Monday and are not shifted.)

FranceAlsaceMoselleCalendar

Behaves exactly like the existing FranceCalendar (all of its holidays, working-day behavior and business-day computations must remain valid), plus these two additional holidays:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)
  • December 26 — Boxing Day / Saint Étienne

This calendar must subclass or otherwise fully reuse FranceCalendar's behavior so that everything FranceCalendar provides continues to work unchanged.

3. workalendar/oceania.py (new module)

AustraliaCalendar (national base calendar)

holidays_set(year) must include:

Fixed-date holidays:

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • January 26 — Australia Day
  • April 25 — ANZAC Day
  • December 25 — Christmas Day

Easter-based holidays:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)

Shift rules for the base national calendar:

  • New Year's Day: if January 1 falls on a weekend, an additional observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2012: Jan 1 is a Sunday → Jan 2 is a holiday).
  • Australia Day: if January 26 falls on a weekend, an additional observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2013: Jan 26 is a Saturday → Jan 28 is a holiday).
  • ANZAC Day: if April 25 falls on a weekend, an additional observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2010: Apr 25 is a Sunday → Apr 26 is a holiday). This ANZAC shift is a distinct, overridable behavior (see subclasses).

Each regional calendar below builds on AustraliaCalendar (it must include all of the national base holidays and shift behavior, except where explicitly overridden) and adds the region-specific holidays listed. Reusable building blocks that appear in several regions:

  • Easter Saturday — the Saturday immediately before Easter Sunday.

General notes on shifting semantics

For all "shift" rules above: the original holiday date always remains in the set; the shift adds an additional observed date. Weekend detection uses Saturday/Sunday. When a holiday falls on a weekend and is shifted, the observed date is the next weekday (typically the following Monday), with cascading to avoid landing on another holiday already claimed (as required by the UK Christmas/Boxing Day case).

02 Add two new calendar classes to the workalendar.core module 241 words · source specification

Overview

Add two new calendar classes to the workalendar.core module: IslamicCalendar and JalaliCalendar. Both classes provide the ability to convert every day of a given Gregorian year into its corresponding date in an alternative calendar system.

Public API Surface

In the module workalendar.core, create the following two classes:

  • IslamicCalendar
  • JalaliCalendar

Both classes must be importable directly from workalendar.core:

from workalendar.core import IslamicCalendar, JalaliCalendar

Both classes must be instantiable with no required arguments:

cal = IslamicCalendar()
cal = JalaliCalendar()

Required Method

Each of the two classes must expose the following instance method:

def converted(self, year):
    ...
Behavior
  • Input: year — an integer representing a Gregorian year (e.g. 2013).
  • Output: A sequence (e.g. a list) containing one entry for each day of the given Gregorian year. Each entry corresponds to a single day of that Gregorian year converted into the target calendar system (Islamic/Hijri for IslamicCalendar, Jalali/Persian for JalaliCalendar).
  • The length of the returned sequence must equal the number of days in the given Gregorian year. For a common (non-leap) year such as 2013, this length is 365.

Notes

  • The two classes may share the underlying conversion mechanism but must expose independent, distinct converter behavior appropriate to their respective calendar systems.
  • The existing Calendar and LunarCalendar classes, and the day-of-week constants MON, TUE, THU, FRI, must remain importable from workalendar.core as before.
