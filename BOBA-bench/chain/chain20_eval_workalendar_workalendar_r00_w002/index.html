01 Across three new/existing modules of the workalendar package, several new public-holiday calendars are to be implemented. 1511 words · source specification, reworded by GLM-5.3-Flash

Task Description

Across three new/existing modules of the workalendar package, several new public-holiday calendars are to be implemented. The framework conventions already in place are followed by all of these calendars: each one is a class that exposes a holidays_set(year) method, and that method returns a set of datetime.date objects for the holidays belonging to that year. Functionality already present on the base classes (holidays_set, add_working_days, working-day computations, weekend/shift helpers) is available for reuse.

Set out below is the exact public API surface along with the behavior that is required.

1. workalendar/africa.py (brand-new module)

SouthAfricaCalendar

holidays_set(year) must contain each of the holidays named below.

Holidays on fixed dates (in every year):

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • March 21 — Human Rights Day
  • April 27 — Freedom Day
  • May 1 — Workers' Day (Labour Day)
  • June 16 — Youth Day
  • August 9 — National Women's Day
  • September 24 — Heritage Day
  • December 16 — Day of Reconciliation
  • December 25 — Christmas Day
  • December 26 — Day of Goodwill

Holidays anchored to Easter:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)

Rule for shifting off a Sunday: Should any holiday from the list above fall on a Sunday, it is observed in addition on the following Monday (and that Monday must likewise be added to the set).

The following concrete consequences must hold:

  • 2013: June 16 (Youth Day) falls on a Sunday; June 17 must therefore be present as well.
  • 2014: April 27 (Freedom Day) falls on a Sunday; April 28 must consequently be present too.

2. workalendar/europe.py (existing module — classes are added here)

UnitedKingdomCalendar

All of the following must be included in holidays_set(year):

Holidays on fixed dates:

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • December 25 — Christmas Day
  • December 26 — Boxing Day

Holidays anchored to Easter:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)
  • Easter Sunday
  • Easter Monday (Monday after Easter Sunday)

Bank holidays with variable dates:

  • Early May Bank Holiday — first Monday of May
  • Spring Bank Holiday — last Monday of May
  • Late Summer Bank Holiday — last Monday of August

Rule for shifting New Year, Christmas and Boxing Day: Whenever a fixed holiday from this list lands on a weekend, an extra observed holiday is inserted on the next weekday that is available, with cascading applied so that no two observed days end up colliding. In practice:

  • January 1 landing on a Sunday makes January 2 a holiday too (2012 is one such example).
  • December 25 being a Sunday: the two dates December 25 and December 26 stay holidays, while December 26 is shifted, in addition, to December 27 (2011 therefore must contain December 25, 26 and 27).

(The May/summer bank holidays are defined on a Monday in every instance and are not shifted.)

FranceAlsaceMoselleCalendar

This calendar behaves exactly as the existing FranceCalendar does (all of its holidays, its working-day behavior and its business-day computations must remain valid); in addition, it carries these two holidays:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)
  • December 26 — Boxing Day / Saint Étienne

This calendar must subclass FranceCalendar or, in some other way, reuse FranceCalendar's behavior fully, so that everything FranceCalendar provides continues to work unchanged.

3. workalendar/oceania.py (newly added module)

AustraliaCalendar (national base calendar)

holidays_set(year) must contain all of the following:

Holidays on fixed dates:

  • January 1 — New Year's Day
  • January 26 — Australia Day
  • April 25 — ANZAC Day
  • December 25 — Christmas Day

Holidays anchored to Easter:

  • Good Friday (Friday before Easter Sunday)

Rules for shifting on the national base calendar:

  • New Year's Day: when January 1 falls on a weekend, one extra observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2012: Jan 1 is a Sunday → Jan 2 is a holiday).
  • Australia Day: when January 26 falls on a weekend, one extra observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2013: Jan 26 is a Saturday → Jan 28 is a holiday).
  • ANZAC Day: when April 25 falls on a weekend, one extra observed day is added on the following Monday (e.g. 2010: Apr 25 is a Sunday → Apr 26 is a holiday). This shift for ANZAC Day is a distinct behavior, and it is overridable (see subclasses).
Regional calendars

Each regional calendar below takes AustraliaCalendar as its starting point (it must include all of the national base holidays and the shift behavior, except where an explicit override applies) and then adds the region-specific holidays as listed. Several regions make use of these shared building blocks:

  • Easter Monday — the Monday immediately after Easter Sunday.
  • Queen's Birthday — second Monday of June.
  • Labour Day (October) — first Monday of October.
  • Boxing Day — December 26.

Shifting semantics — general notes

Across all of the "shift" rules above: the date of the original holiday remains in the set at all times; the shift contributes one additional observed date. For weekend detection, Saturday/Sunday is what gets checked. Once a holiday falls on a weekend and is shifted, the observed date is the next weekday (typically the Monday that follows), with cascading so as to avoid any holiday that has already been claimed (the UK Christmas/Boxing Day case is what requires this).

02 Two new calendar classes, IslamicCalendar and JalaliCalendar, are to be added to the workalendar.core module. 263 words · source specification, reworded by GLM-5.3-Flash

Summary

Two new calendar classes, IslamicCalendar and JalaliCalendar, are to be added to the workalendar.core module. Both classes offer the capability to convert every day of a given Gregorian year into the date corresponding to it in an alternative calendar system.

Public API

Create the following two classes in the module workalendar.core:

  • IslamicCalendar
  • JalaliCalendar

It must be possible to import both classes directly from workalendar.core:

from workalendar.core import IslamicCalendar, JalaliCalendar

Both classes must support instantiation with no required arguments:

cal = IslamicCalendar()
cal = JalaliCalendar()

Required Method

The following instance method must be exposed by each of the two classes:

def converted(self, year):
    ...
Behavior
  • Input: year — an integer that represents a Gregorian year (e.g. 2013).
  • Output: A sequence (e.g. a list) that contains one entry for each day of the given Gregorian year. Each entry corresponds to a single day of that Gregorian year, converted into the target calendar system (Islamic/Hijri for IslamicCalendar, Jalali/Persian for JalaliCalendar).
  • The length of the sequence that is returned must equal the number of days in the given Gregorian year. In the case of a common (non-leap) year such as 2013, this length is 365.

Notes

  • The two classes may share the underlying conversion mechanism but must expose converter behavior that is independent, distinct, and appropriate to their respective calendar systems.
  • The existing Calendar and LunarCalendar classes, and also the day-of-week constants MON, TUE, THU, FRI, must continue to be importable from workalendar.core as before.

Given a Gregorian year, this method returns a sequence of converted date values. When a call is made with year=2013:

from workalendar.america import MexicoCalendar

The class must be instantiable with no arguments, and it must offer a method:

holidays_set(year)

This method returns a set of datetime.date objects that stand for the holidays observed for the calendar year given to it.

Holidays With Fixed Dates

Given a year, the returned set must contain the following holidays (using that year's values):

  • New Year's Day — January 1
  • Constitution Day — February 4
  • Benito Juárez's Birthday — March 18
  • Labour Day — May 1
  • Independence Day — September 16
  • Revolution Day — November 18
  • Christmas Day — December 25

(When one of these dates falls on its nominal day, that specific date must appear. Verified example: for the year 2013, all seven of the dates appear — Jan 1, Feb 4, Mar 18, May 1, Sep 16, Nov 18, Dec 25.)

Rules for Weekend Shifts

A shift is applied to certain holidays when they land on a weekend. The shifting covers New Year's Day (Jan 1), Labour Day (May 1), and Christmas Day (Dec 25).

The rules governing the shift are:

  1. If the holiday falls on a Sunday, the observed date is shifted to the following Monday. The shifted (Monday) date must appear in the holiday set of the year that contains that Monday.

  2. If the holiday falls on a Saturday, the observed date is shifted to the preceding Friday. The shifted (Friday) date must appear in the holiday set of the year that contains that Friday.

A key point about which year's set ends up holding the shifted date: the holiday set that receives the shifted observed date is the one belonging to the year for which holidays are being computed, and the basis for this is the nominal holiday tied to that computation. Specifically:

  • While the holidays of a year are being computed, a holiday whose nominal date is a Sunday must add the Monday that follows it. (Example: for the 2017 computation, Jan 1 2017 falls on a Sunday, so Jan 2 2017 is added. For the 2016 computation: Dec 25 2016 falls on a Sunday, so Dec 26 2016 is added; May 1 2016 falls on a Sunday, so May 2 2016 is added.)

  • While the holidays of a year are being computed, if a holiday nominally belonging to the following year would land on a Saturday, the Friday observed for it (which lands in the year currently being computed) must be included in the set of the current year. (Example: for the 2021 computation, Jan 1 2022 falls on a Saturday, so Dec 31 2021 is added; Dec 25 2022 falls on a Saturday, so Dec 24 2021 is added. For the same-year Labour Day as well: May 1 2021 falls on a Saturday, so Apr 30 2021 is added.)

To sum up: in the shift-to-Friday case, a holiday whose nominal date lies in year N+1 may end up contributing an observed date (the preceding Friday) that falls in year N, and year N's holiday set must contain that Friday.

from workalendar.africa import IvoryCoastCalendar
